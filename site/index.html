03 / START WITH THE GUIDE

A guided setup.
An informed decision.

The terminal guide explains each stage, checks the device, and asks for a separate confirmation before each risky action.

Unlocking can erase your setup. Flashing can permanently brick your device. Back up your data and read the recovery and stopping conditions first. Recovery is not guaranteed.

  1. 01

    Identify your device

    Match the model, firmware, bootloader and starting state for your chosen workflow. The original unlock needs stock slot B; the upgrade needs the covered existing root.

  2. 02

    Get the essentials ready

    A Linux x86_64 computer, Python 3.10+, network ADB access, normal device power, and enough backup space. Unlock experiments also require a USB drive with a FAT32 first partition.

  3. 03

    Read, then run the guide

    The menu separates original unlock/root, the reviewed upgrade, and the untested new-firmware unlock. Read its dedicated instructions and risk notices first.

04 / BEFORE YOU BEGIN

A few good
questions.

Know what this project covers before you change your device.

Is this for the 2023 onn. 4K?

No. The 2023 onn. 4K non-Pro is a different device. This guide is for the original 2024 onn. 4K Pro (jarvis / SNA), pictured above. Use the supported model’s product listing and the firmware checks to identify your box.

Does the new 2026 4K Pro work?

No support is provided for the new 2026 hardware. It is untested. The 2026 security patches and test dates refer to software and testing on the earlier 2024 model.

Will I lose my apps and settings?

Unlocking required a factory reset on the tested unit, erasing apps, accounts, and settings. The later upgrade to 16202347 preserved that setup and root. Fresh unlocking on 16202347 is untested; expect data loss and possible permanent bricking. Back up anything you need before starting.

Is permanent root actually verified?

Yes, on the single tested unit: root returned after a normal reboot with the thumb drive physically removed. The original measurements include the root identity, partition hashes, and Magisk environment checks. This does not establish repeatability on another unit or long-term reliability.

What about 16202347 or slot A?

The specific rooted 15051976/B → 16202347/A upgrade was verified on one device. A separate fresh-unlock option for locked 16202347 on existing slot A or B is untested and can permanently brick the device; you take sole responsibility for that risk. Other builds remain unsupported. Do not downgrade, switch slots, or bypass checks to force compatibility.

Can I use Windows, macOS, or a shared patched image?

The documented host is Linux x86_64; other host systems are untested. The boot image must be patched on your own target device, following the guide. Do not use another person’s patched image.
